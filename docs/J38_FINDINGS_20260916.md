(host PENL0397, assembly `264MN024625A01/A`, 414 unique prototypes).

## Verdict

**NX_FILE_CLEAN - 414 / 414 parts.** The native NX files pushed to the
customer carry exactly one attribute per business field, always under the
canonical title. The duplicates seen in the customer Teamcenter are created
by the customer's TC conversion/import process, not by the NX journals and
not by the pushed files.

## Hand the customer

1. **The mapping contract.** Each business attribute lives in NX under
   category `WAEItem` with exactly these titles, one value each:
   `Commodity_Code`, `COMMODITYTYPE`, `Country_of_Origin`,
   `Export_Control_Number`, `MFG`, `MPN`, `Stocking_Type`,
   `Unit_Of_Measure`, `WAE_Hazardous`, `WAE_VERSION` (plus
   `COMPONENT_CLASS`, `LIFED`, `NX_FINISH`, `SERIAL_NUMBERED_PART`,
   `Serviceable_item_flag`, `Temperature_Sensitive`). Strings like
   "Commodity Code" or "Mfr. Name" are NX **display aliases only** - they
   are not stored in the file and must not be used as TC property keys.
2. **Where to look on their side.** Their conversion duplicates the fields,
   so they should check:
   - does their importer write both a BMIDE-mapped property (localized
     display name) *and* a second property/form field keyed by the raw
     NX title?
   - does their item type genuinely define two properties per field (one
     localized, one named like the raw title)?
   - or are they comparing the ItemRevision mapped properties against the
     raw CAD-attributes page of the NX dataset? Those are two different
     storages showing the same value by design - not a duplicate write.
3. **Optional marker test.** Push one part with a unique marker value in
   `Commodity_Code` only; ask them to report every TC field that receives
   it. That pinpoints the second write path in one round trip.
